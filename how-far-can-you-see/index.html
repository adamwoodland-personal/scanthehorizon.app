How far can you see? The distance to the horizon

From your eyes, 1.7 m above the sea, the horizon is about 5.0 km away. Climb higher and it moves out fast: about 38 km from 100 m up, and 121 km from the top of a 1,000 m mountain.

Can you see a mountain that far away?

Beyond the horizon, the curve of the Earth hides the lower part of anything further off. A distant mountain shows only if it is taller than the part hidden at its distance.

138 m hidden by the curveThe top 362 m of it shows above the horizon.

For example, from a beach (eyes 1.7 m up), the curve hides the lowest 138 m of a mountain 50 km away, so a 500 m mountain shows its top 362 m. The worked formula: the hidden height is (d − d₀)² × (1 − k) ÷ 2R, where d is the distance to the mountain, d₀ your distance to the horizon, R the Earth’s radius (6,371 km) and k = 0.13 the refraction coefficient.

How refraction lets you see further

Light from far away bends slightly downwards as it passes through the air, which is denser near the ground. That lets you see a little over the curve: with the usual coefficient of 0.13, the horizon is about 7% further away than a straight line would reach. On some days the air bends light more, which is why a coast or island at the edge of view can appear one day and not the next.

Questions

How far away is the horizon?

For someone standing at the water’s edge, with their eyes about 1.7 m up, the sea horizon is about 5.0 km away (4.7 km if light travelled in straight lines). The higher you are, the further it is: about 38 km from 100 m up and 121 km from 1,000 m.

How far can you see from a mountain?

From the top of a 1,000 m mountain the sea horizon is about 121 km away, and another mountain further off can show above it: its top clears the curve if it is taller than the part the curve hides at that distance. The calculator above works that out.

Why can I sometimes see further than the numbers say?

Air bends light slightly downwards, so you see a little beyond the straight-line horizon. These figures use a refraction coefficient of 0.13, as Scan the Horizon does, but the real bending changes with the weather, so a distant coast or island can come and go from day to day.
